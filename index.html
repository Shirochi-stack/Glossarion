How it works

From raw chapters to a finished book in four steps.

Glossarion runs on your computer or phone. Your files stay with you, and text only goes to the AI you pick.

01

Drop in a file

EPUB, TXT, PDF, Markdown, subtitles, images, CBZ manga, SDLXLIFF, even RPG Maker games.

02

Pick any model

Your API key, a ChatGPT / Grok / Claude / Gemini login, Ollama on your PC or a model on your phone.

03

Lock names & terms

An automatic glossary keeps names, genders and terms consistent from the first chapter to the last.

04

Read, check, export

Get a rebuilt EPUB or PDF, read it in the built-in library and let the QA scanner catch mistakes.

Features

Everything paid AI translation sites offer. Free.

Glossarion isn't here to replace translators. It's a free, open-source alternative to subscription AI translation services: use your own API key or local model directly, with no subscription on top and no markup on your usage.

📖 Novel translation

Whole books, chapter after chapter, with context.

Load an EPUB, TXT or PDF and translate it end to end. Glossarion carries recent chapters (or a rolling summary) into every request, so tone and story stay coherent, and splits long chapters into chunks automatically.

  • Parallel chapters, automatic retries and key failover
  • Stop any time and resume exactly where you left off
  • Prompt profiles for Korean, Japanese and Chinese — edit them or write your own
  • Rebuilt EPUB keeps images, cover and structure; titles and metadata are translated too

🎌 Manga translation

Clean, typeset manga and webtoon pages.

Glossarion finds every speech bubble, reads the text, removes it from the art and letters the translation back in. The AI sees the whole page, so expressions and context shape the wording.

  • Bubble detection with RT-DETR and YOLO models
  • OCR from your vision model, Google, Azure, RapidOCR, manga-ocr and more
  • Local inpainting (LaMa, AOT, anime) or a cloud image-edit model
  • Fonts, colours, outlines, webtoon mode, a page editor and CBZ output

📓 Glossary generation

Names, genders and terms that never drift.

Glossarion builds a glossary from your book before or during translation and sends only the terms each chapter needs. Characters keep one spelling and the right pronouns, and techniques and places keep one name.

  • Automatic extraction with eight modes, from minimal to full
  • Glossary Manager: filter, find & replace, undo, de-duplicate, resolve gender conflicts
  • A unified glossary per language pair, auto-matched to each of your books
  • Optional NER name check flags and fixes misspelled names in the output
  • Build a glossary from a raw book and an existing translation of it

🔑 Multiple API keys & instances

Many keys, many books, all at once.

Add as many keys as you like and Glossarion rotates through them, cools down any key that hits a rate limit and fails over on errors. Give keys an instance number and several books translate side by side, each with its own keys, log and progress.

  • Separate pools for translation, fallback, glossary, QA, image editing and more
  • A per-key endpoint, model, parameters and schedule
  • A fallback pool retries refused content on another model
  • Keys are encrypted on your device

🦙 Local LLMs

Run it all offline, on your own hardware.

Type ollamapull/qwen3.5:9b and Glossarion installs Ollama, downloads the model and starts translating — no setup and no bill. Already running Ollama or LM Studio? Use ollama/ or lmstudio/, or point any prefix at your own OpenAI-compatible server.

  • On Android and iOS, GGUF models run on the phone itself through llama.cpp
  • Ready-made Qwen3.5 presets from 0.8B to 9B, plus Llama, Gemma and LFM2

  • Nothing leaves your device

👁️ Vision & image editing

Send images, get text — or get new images back.

Every job has an output mode. Vision reads text out of images, scans and image-only EPUBs, then translates it. Image mode sends your pictures to an image-editing or image-generation model; video and audio modes are there too.

  • Image editing through models like nan/gpt-image-2 or any OpenAI-compatible edit endpoint

🛡️ QA scanner

Catch the mistakes before your readers do.

After a run, the QA scanner reads every translated chapter and flags what went wrong, then lets you fix chapters one by one or send them back for retranslation.

  • Duplicate or skipped chapters, including AI Hunter similarity checks
  • Untranslated foreign text, AI "thinking" leftovers and truncated replies
  • Broken HTML, missing images, word-count ratio and glossary drift
  • Runs automatically after translation if you want

FAQ

Questions people ask.

Is Glossarion really free?

Yes. Glossarion is open source under the AGPL-3.0 license and every feature is in every free download. The only possible cost is the AI itself: if you use a paid API, you pay that provider directly. With a local model or a free tier, translating costs nothing.

Do I need an API key?

No. You can run a local model (ollamapull/qwen3.5:9b sets everything up for you), run a model on your phone, sign in with a ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini or Grok account, or use free tiers such as a Google AI Studio key or OpenRouter's free models. See Pick a model.

Which model gives the best translations?

Large frontier models (Gemini Pro, Claude, GPT, DeepSeek, Kimi, GLM) give the most natural prose. For free, Gemini Flash Lite on a free key is a strong start, and Qwen3.5 9B is a good local model if your PC has the memory. You can switch models at any time.

How does it keep names consistent?

A glossary. Glossarion extracts characters, places and terms from the book (with gender, so pronouns stay right) and sends the relevant entries with each chapter. The optional NER name check then scans the output for misspelled names and fixes them.

Is my data private?

Glossarion runs on your own device and has no server of its own. Your text goes only to the AI provider you choose — or nowhere at all with a local model. API keys are encrypted at rest.

What about mature content?

Glossarion doesn't add its own filters or word limits; what a model will translate is up to its provider. If a model refuses a passage, a fallback key pool can retry it on a different model automatically. Local models have no provider policy at all.

Why isn't it on the App Store or Google Play?

Glossarion is a free community project, and every build is published on GitHub. On Android you install the APK directly; on iPhone and iPad you sideload the IPA with a tool like SideStore or AltStore. See the install guide.
